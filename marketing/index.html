SIGN IN ONCE TO SHARE

Less signing in. More getting on.

Enable Claude sync on both Macs, then Share Sign-In once per subscription. The official Claude Code sign-in creates a reusable token stored in each approved Mac's Keychain.

Add an account while away.

A new reusable sign-in works on that Mac immediately. When LAN or VPN returns, it reaches your approved Macs automatically. Received accounts work even while the originating Mac is offline. Approved Macs can pass shared accounts to their other explicitly approved sync Macs.

Keep the right account in the right tab.

Use the account picker in the status bar. Your choice applies to the next Claude launch in that tab; other tabs and already running Claude processes keep their account.

Ordinary local sign-ins need a one-time Share Sign-In before they can sync. Claude.ai connectors still need a normal local sign-in. No artificial account limit.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN

Your Macs connect.
No TerminalDB cloud in between.

No TerminalDB cloud account is needed. No browser terminal, public discovery service, vendor relay, or public terminal port. Your VPN is how you reach home from elsewhere.

Read the security model↗
01

Verify the Mac, not just its name.

Compare the full fingerprints and approve on both Macs. Connections use TLS 1.3 with mutually verified, pinned device keys.

02

Pairing and account sync are different choices.

Pairing allows workstation access. Claude sync and AWS sync each need their own approval on both ends. New paired Macs do not silently receive accounts.

03

Make a deliberate trust decision.

A paired terminal grants powerful access to your macOS user. Remove a Mac to block it here. Stopping sync prevents future transfers; revoke credentials at the provider to invalidate copies already received.

Security depends on verified pairing, correct implementation, and uncompromised Macs. Claude requests and software updates have their own network activity; terminal connectivity goes directly to your configured Macs.

A FEW PRACTICAL DETAILS

Clear answers.
Fewer surprises.

Do I need an account to use TerminalDB?

No. Start a local terminal immediately. Pairing your own Macs is optional. Claude and AWS features use your existing provider accounts and need internet for provider sign-in and requests.

What happens when I close my laptop?

Its views disconnect. Work on another awake, logged-in Mac continues in that Mac's session host. Wake your laptop and restore LAN or VPN connectivity to reconnect. A sleeping execution Mac cannot keep commands running; reboot, logout, or session-host failure ends its sessions.

Can I use this away from my LAN?

Yes, through your own VPN with a route to the execution Mac's private network. Pair initially on the same LAN. If Bonjour discovery does not cross your VPN, save the paired Mac's internal IP address or internal DNS name in its Connection settings. TerminalDB does not set up your VPN, forward router ports, relay traffic, or synchronize files.

Will an account I add while away appear when I get home?

Yes, if it is a reusable Claude sign-in shared with those Macs. It works on the originating Mac first and syncs to approved Macs after LAN or VPN returns. Renewals and removals also propagate on reconnect. Ordinary local Claude sign-ins need the one-time Share Sign-In flow before they can be shared.

What if two Macs have different AWS profiles, or the same profile name?

TerminalDB detects each Mac's supported profiles. Shared identity includes the provider, account, and role or principal; a matching name alone is not enough. On a receiving Mac, keep a familiar name or choose an alias. Review conflicts before a local profile changes. Unrelated profiles stay untouched.

Can AWS access work while the source Mac is offline?

Yes, until its temporary credentials expire. Renewal needs a reachable Mac with a valid sign-in. Otherwise, use Sign In on This Mac. Receiving tools must support AWS's credential_process interface. A managed same-name profile uses TerminalDB's Sign In button for future authentication; a separate alias can preserve your original AWS login command.

Does unpairing revoke every account credential?

No. It blocks that Mac on this host and stops future transfers. It cannot erase an offline copy or revoke a token already held by another process. Revoke access with Claude or AWS when immediate invalidation is required. Pairing with one Mac does not authorize access to all of its peers.

Is Claude account sharing the same as copying every login file?

No. TerminalDB shares explicitly enabled reusable tokens, stored in each approved Mac's device-only Keychain. Ordinary rotating Claude login and refresh credentials are not copied. Claude.ai connectors still require a normal local sign-in. Token renewal may require restarting Claude, and provider-side expiry or revocation still applies.
